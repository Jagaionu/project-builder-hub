# Glossary

## What is a VRID?

A VRID (Vehicle Run ID) is the identifier for a single planned route — one driver's run with an origin warehouse, a destination, a planned start time, and a sequence of stops. On the Dispatch board each route/job is a VRID. Manually created routes and bulk-imported routes are both VRIDs.

## What is a tour?

A tour is a route with more than one leg/stop grouped together. On the dispatch board a job tagged "Tour ·2" means it has 2 linked legs.

## What does CPT and CIT mean?

- **CPT** — Critical Pull Time: the latest time a load can leave a stop and still be on time.
- **CIT** — Critical Induct Time: the latest time a load can be inducted/arrive and still be on time.

These appear next to planned dock/yard times on a route's stops.

## What does "off shift" mean?

A driver is OFF_SHIFT when they are not currently working — outside their shift hours, on a day off, or on holiday. Off-shift drivers are not shown as active on the live map.

## What is the tachograph?

The tachograph is the legally required record of a driver's actual driving time. The app sends drivers a weekly tachograph request to log their real driving minutes, which is compared against the system estimate.

## What is a "global" warehouse?

A global warehouse is a shared location available to every company, created by the platform super admin. A company also has its own warehouses. Company warehouses count toward the plan's warehouse limit; global ones do not.

## Connected vs Active vs On route (Live Map)?

- **Connected** — the driver's app is online and sending location.
- **Active** — the driver is on shift and working.
- **On route** — the driver is currently assigned to and running a route.
